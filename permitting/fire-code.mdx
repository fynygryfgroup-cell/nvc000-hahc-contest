Four of the nine Permitting Office violations documented in this opposition record arise directly from fire code requirements under the International Residential Code — requirements that are entirely independent of the Houston Heights Design Guidelines and HAHC design review. The north (plat "East") wall is drawn at 3'-6 1/2" from the property line on the applicant's own submitted site plan, placing it squarely within the IRC Table R302.1 fire-rating trigger zone. The garage apartment, equipped with a full kitchen, full bathroom, and sleeping area, constitutes a second dwelling unit under any reasonable code interpretation — triggering fire separation and independent egress requirements that the submittals do not address. When the boundary dispute is layered onto these violations, every distance measurement narrows and every fire code threshold becomes harder to satisfy.

## How the Boundary Dispute Compounds Every Fire Code Issue

Before addressing each violation individually, it is important to understand how the active boundary dispute between 1501 and 1505 Tulane Street interacts with fire code compliance.

The applicant's own site plan (A01, Sheet 17) shows the north (plat "East") side wall at 3'-6 1/2" from the property line. This measurement is already within the IRC fire-rating trigger zone for a 1-hour assembly. However, the submitted drawings are based on a boundary position that is disputed. The recorded plats from 1996, 2001, and 2007 — every survey in the chain except the applicant's new survey — place the southern property line of 1505 Tulane Street materially further north than the applicant claims, using found ½" and ⅝" iron rod control monuments.

If the true property line is restored to its historically monumented position — or if the adverse possession claim up to the 2009 retaining wall is recognized — the effective setback from the applicant's wall to the true property line shrinks. For fire code purposes, every inch of that shrinkage matters:

- A wall at **3'-6 1/2"** already requires a **1-hour fire-rated assembly** under IRC Table R302.1.
- A wall at **less than 3'-0"** requires a **1-hour fire-rated assembly with 0% openings** — no windows, no doors.
- A wall at **0"** (on the property line) requires fire-rated construction on both sides and may trigger additional IBC requirements depending on occupancy classification.

The Permitting Office cannot verify compliance with IRC Table R302.1 until the true property line is established through an independent RPLS survey accounting for all found control monuments.

## P-1: Fire-Rated Wall Construction Required (IRC Table R302.1)

The following reproduces Section V-B, P-1 of the full opposition report verbatim.

<Warning>
**P-1 — Fire-Rated Wall Construction Required (IRC Table R302.1)**

The proposed site plan shows the addition's north (plat "East") side wall at **3'-6 1/2"** from the north (plat "East") property line. Under IRC Table R302.1, any exterior wall located less than 5 feet from a property line must have a **1-hour fire-resistance rating** (tested per ASTM E119/UL 263) from both sides. Walls located less than 3 feet from a property line generally permit **0% openings** — no unprotected windows or doors.

The submitted elevation drawings show standard wall construction and window placements along the north (plat "East") elevation. If this wall is within 3'-6 1/2" of the property line as drawn, the drawings fail to incorporate the required fire-rated assemblies and may show prohibited openings. The Permitting Office should flag this during plan review.

This issue is compounded by the boundary dispute: if the true property line is even closer than the applicant claims, the fire separation distance shrinks further, increasing the fire-rating requirements.
</Warning>

## P-2: Garage Apartment Constitutes a Second Dwelling Unit

The following reproduces Section V-B, P-2 of the full opposition report verbatim.

<Warning>
**P-2 — Garage Apartment Constitutes a Second Dwelling Unit**

The garage apartment includes a full bathroom (tub/shower, vanity, toilet), a kitchen (countertop, sink, appliance spaces), and a living/sleeping area with a closet. This constitutes a **complete, independent dwelling unit** — an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) or second dwelling unit.

If the garage apartment is permitted as a separate dwelling unit (rather than a guest suite within a single-family home), building codes require:
- **1-hour fire separation** between the two dwelling units (IRC R302.3) — floor/ceiling and wall assemblies separating the main house from the garage apartment.
- **Independent egress** — each unit must have an independent, direct path of egress to the exterior without traveling through the other unit's private spaces.

The submittals do not indicate whether fire separation assemblies are specified between the dwelling units, or whether the garage apartment has independent egress. The Permitting Office should verify the occupancy classification and require appropriate fire separation.
</Warning>

### Boundary Dispute Interaction — P-2

The second dwelling unit classification is independent of the boundary dispute — the garage apartment's full kitchen, bathroom, and sleeping area constitute a second dwelling unit regardless of where the property line falls. However, the boundary dispute compounds the fire separation issue: the 1-hour fire-rated assemblies required between dwelling units (IRC R302.3) and the fire-rated wall assemblies required at the property line (IRC R302.1) must both be satisfied simultaneously. The Permitting Office must evaluate the interaction between these two requirements, particularly along the north wall, where the structure is simultaneously within the fire-code proximity zone of the property line and required to provide inter-unit fire separation.

## P-5: Egress from Garage Apartment

The following reproduces Section V-B, P-5 of the full opposition report verbatim.

<Warning>
**P-5 — Egress from Garage Apartment**

Occupants of the garage apartment's second floor must currently travel through the **enclosed gallery connector** and use the **main house's central stairway** to reach the ground level. The Permitting Office should verify that the garage apartment has code-compliant emergency escape windows (EERO) opening directly to the exterior, meeting IRC Section R310 minimums:

- Minimum **5.7 sq ft** net clear opening
- Minimum **24"** height
- Minimum **20"** width
- Maximum **44"** sill height
</Warning>

Occupants of the garage apartment's second floor must currently travel through the enclosed gallery connector and use the main house's central stairway to reach the ground level. The Permitting Office should verify that the garage apartment has code-compliant emergency escape windows (EERO) opening directly to the exterior, meeting IRC Section R310 minimums (minimum 5.7 sq ft net clear opening, min. 24" height, min. 20" width, max. 44" sill height).

### Boundary Dispute Interaction — P-5

The egress deficiency is, at its core, a design problem independent of the boundary dispute. However, the boundary dispute may constrain the available solutions. If the true property line is closer to the north wall than the applicant claims, any egress window opening on the north elevation must still comply with IRC R302.1 requirements for openings within 3 to 5 feet of a property line — which may prohibit or restrict the placement of emergency egress openings on that elevation. This means the boundary dispute could make it **more difficult, not easier**, to achieve code-compliant egress without a fundamental redesign of the garage apartment floor plan and the connecting gallery.
