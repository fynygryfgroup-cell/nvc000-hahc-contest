## Four Required Permit and Regulatory Requirements

The four regulatory requirements that apply if soil boring or geotechnical drilling has been performed at 1505 Tulane Street are set out below, reproduced verbatim from the full opposition report.

### Requirement 1 — Monitoring Well/Boring Permit (Chapter 40, Article XII)

**Monitoring Well/Boring Permit (Chapter 40, Article XII):** If any boring was conducted in the public right-of-way (street, sidewalk, or easement), a Monitoring Well/Boring Permit from the City Engineer is required. This permit requires an application, a GIMS utility map, insurance certificates, a surety bond ($1,000 for the first facility, $750 each additional), and a fee.

### Requirement 2 — Geotechnical Consultant Responsibilities

**Geotechnical Consultant Responsibilities:** The City of Houston's Geotechnical and Environmental Requirements place the burden on the Geotechnical Consultant to "ensure necessary permits are obtained before commencement of drilling" and to use the property owner's help to locate underground utilities before drilling begins. Drilling cannot start until utility lines are cleared and marked.

### Requirement 3 — Texas 811 Utility Notification

<Note>
**Texas 811 Call Required Before Any Drilling**

At least 48 hours before any drilling, **Texas 811 must be called** to mark underground utilities. Soil borings of even 4–6 feet can strike utility lines. This requirement applies regardless of whether the drilling is on private property or in the public right-of-way.
</Note>

**Texas 811 Utility Notification:** At least 48 hours before any drilling, Texas 811 must be called to mark underground utilities. Soil borings of even 4–6 feet can strike utility lines.

### Requirement 4 — Trespass Concern Under Texas Penal Code § 30.05

<Warning>
**Criminal Trespass — Texas Penal Code § 30.05**

If any soil sampling was conducted on the **adjacent 1501 Tulane St. property** or on the **disputed boundary strip** without the property owner's written permission, it constitutes **criminal trespass under Texas Penal Code § 30.05**. Texas courts have specifically held that conducting geophysical operations or soil sampling on someone's land is a "valuable property right which belongs exclusively to the owner."

The disputed boundary strip — a 3- to 5-foot area along the shared property line — is the subject of an active boundary dispute. Until that dispute is resolved through an independent RPLS survey, neither party can authorize geotechnical operations on that strip. Any drilling on that strip without the 1501 Tulane St. property owner's written permission may constitute trespass regardless of which party's boundary claim ultimately prevails.
</Warning>

**Trespass Concern:** If any soil sampling was conducted on the adjacent 1501 Tulane St. property or on the disputed boundary strip without the property owner's written permission, it constitutes criminal trespass under Texas Penal Code § 30.05. Texas courts have specifically held that conducting geophysical operations or soil sampling on someone's land is a "valuable property right which belongs exclusively to the owner."

## Conclusion and Permitting Office Request

The absence of any permit record in the City's database suggests that if soil drilling has occurred, it may have been done without the required permits. The Permitting Office should investigate whether geotechnical work has been performed on the site without proper authorization, and whether any such work encroached onto the neighboring 1501 Tulane St. property.

The full context of the boundary dispute — including the active adverse possession and boundary-by-acquiescence claims, the survey pin embedded in the street at the retaining wall line, and the chain of recorded plats establishing the historic boundary — is documented in detail in the [Boundary Dispute Analysis](/boundary/survey-chain) section of this opposition record.
