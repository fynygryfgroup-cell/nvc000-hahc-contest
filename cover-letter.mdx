This is the formal cover letter submitted by Anthony Leavitt & LEAVITTHAAS LLC to the Houston Archaeological and Historical Commission on September 24, 2026, on behalf of the adjacent property owners at 1501 Tulane Street. It introduces the enclosed opposition report and formally requests that the Commission deny or continue to defer both COA applications for 1505 Tulane Street until the boundary dispute, lot combination issue, and all internal submittal inconsistencies are fully resolved. Every word of this letter is reproduced verbatim from the original submission.

## Letter Text

Dear Commissioners and Permitting Staff:

Please accept the enclosed report as formal opposition to the Certificate of Appropriateness applications for 1505 Tulane Street, submitted under file numbers HP2026-0244 (Garage & Apartment) and HP2026-0248 (Main House Addition & Rehabilitation).

We are the adjacent property owners at 1501 Tulane Street, located immediately to the south of the subject property. Our family has owned and occupied this home continuously since 2009, and our family members owned it from 2000 to 2009 — a combined occupancy of over 25 years.

At the September 10, 2026 hearing, the Commission deferred these applications after we raised an active boundary dispute on the shared southern property line. The enclosed report documents the full factual and regulatory record supporting our request that the Commission deny or continue to defer these applications until the following are resolved:

---

**Active boundary dispute — with a fluctuating property line.** The applicant has unilaterally shifted the southern property line south by an estimated 3 to 5 feet in the submittal drawings, contradicting recorded plats from 1996, 2000/2001, 2007, and 2016. The applicant's new survey is the only plat in the entire chain that departs from the established dimensions, and the survey team was reportedly sent back a second time after the owner was dissatisfied with their initial results. Critically, the southern property line continues to fluctuate across the submitted plans — at one point showing the rear of 509 W. 15th Street (a separately owned, separately platted property) as being on the applicant's property. Additionally, a survey pin embedded in the street, marked with orange survey paint, is located at the historic retaining wall line — independent physical evidence that corroborates our family's boundary claim.

<Warning>
  **Unapproved lot combination — and an illegal build across the lot line.** Lots 16 and 17 are being treated as a single 8,799 sq ft parcel for FAR and lot coverage calculations, but no replat or Agreement to Bind Properties has been recorded. If the lots remain legally separate, the parcel with the house would be automatically disqualified. On the southern lot, the plans represent a house where the existing garage currently stands — obscuring the fact that the project is building across an un-replatted interior lot line. The south lot's garage and garage apartment are accessory structures with no main residence on that lot — an impermissible configuration.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **Cumulative side setback violation (15-foot rule).** The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require a minimum cumulative side setback of 15 feet for two-story houses. The submitted drawings show approximately 12 feet cumulative (3'-6 1/2" north (plat "East") + 8'-5 15/16" south (plat "West")) — a face-of-drawing violation that is 3 feet below the minimum.
</Warning>

**Per-lot FAR violations.** When evaluated on a per-lot basis (as required without a recorded replat), the north lot's main house FAR of ~2,500–3,300+ sq ft far exceeds the 40% maximum (~1,760 sq ft cap on a ~4,400 sq ft lot). The south lot's garage (400+ sq ft) and garage apartment (600+ sq ft) total ~1,000–1,133 sq ft — numerically under the cap, but impermissible as an accessory structure without a main residence.

**Driveway retention violation.** The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require historic homes with existing concrete or asphalt driveways to retain those driveways during renovation. The new plan does not retain the existing driveway.

<Warning>
  **Face-of-drawing setback violations.** The proposed site plan drawing itself shows a north (plat "East") side setback of 3'-6 1/2" — below the mandatory 5' minimum — and a west/rear (plat "North") setback of 1'-0 1/8" to the addition, contradicting the narrative claim of "24' at least."
</Warning>

**Garage classified as "detached" but structurally attached.** The so-called "unconditioned breezeway" is actually an enclosed, conditioned gallery with an elevator connecting the house to the garage apartment. The roof plan shows a continuous roofline. This invalidates the 528 sq ft detached garage exemption and pushes the project over the 40% FAR cap.

**Internal inconsistencies and arithmetic errors.** The submittals contain contradictory existing-home square footage (2,192 vs. 1,299 sq ft), a ridge height discrepancy, a lot coverage arithmetic error, and a 196 sq ft discrepancy between the drawn garage footprint (~484 sq ft) and the claimed footprint (680 sq ft).

**Context area misrepresentation.** The submittals label neighboring properties as "2 STY" to justify the full 2-story massing, but the labels are misleading: 1501 and 1515 Tulane are camelbacks (1-story bungalows with a smaller subordinate 2-story rear portion — a historic Houston Heights form), and 1511 Tulane is a true 1-story with no camelback at all. The applicant's proposed design is a full 2-story — fundamentally different from a camelback. The only true full 2-story in the context area (1530 Ashland) is on a different block and was built before the historic board existed. A full 2-story contributing structure behind the property that shares a northern plat line with 1505 was omitted entirely. This misrepresentation makes the full 2-story massing appear compatible when the Tulane block actually consists of 1-story homes and camelbacks.

**Texas property law protections.** Under Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code §§ 16.021 and 16.026, the common-law doctrine of boundary by acquiescence (*Gulf Oil Corp. v. Marathon Oil Co.*), and the principles established in *Schiele v. Kimball*, 194 S.W. 944 (Tex. 1917), the 2009 retaining wall on our northern boundary — with its concrete footings, structural posts, and wood-overlay reinforcement — establishes a viable adverse possession and boundary-by-acquiescence claim to the 4–5 foot strip.

**Permitting Office violations (building code, fire code, life safety).** Beyond the Historic Board issues, the submittals contain numerous violations of the City of Houston Building Code (IRC/IBC): the north (plat "East") wall at 3'-6 1/2" requires 1-hour fire-rated construction that is not shown; the garage apartment constitutes a second dwelling unit requiring fire separation and independent egress that are not addressed; the house raising to 32" requires a house-moving permit and structural engineering plans that are not included; garage fire separation requirements (Type X gypsum, fire-rated doors) are not specified; egress from the garage apartment may depend on the main house stairway; no foundation plans, structural calculations, or windstorm/flood documentation are included in the submittals; and the residential elevator requires a separate permit and ASME compliance.

**Soil drilling without a permit.** A search of the City of Houston's Online Sold Permit database reveals that no building permits have been pulled for 1505 Tulane or 1505 1/2 Tulane — under any address variation or under the owner's name. If soil borings or geotechnical drilling has been performed on the property without the required Monitoring Well/Boring Permit (Chapter 40, Article XII), this constitutes unpermitted work. If any soil sampling was conducted on the disputed boundary strip or the neighboring 1501 Tulane St. property without the owner's written permission, it also constitutes criminal trespass under Texas Penal Code § 30.05.

**Two COA signs confirm the HAHC recognizes separate lots.** Two separate COA signs are posted on the property — one for the garage (HP2026-0244) and one for the main house addition (HP2026-0248). The presence of two separate COA applications, each with its own sign, strongly suggests the HAHC's own processing system treats these as distinct lots with distinct structures. This creates an internal contradiction: the HAHC's COA process recognizes two separate lots, yet the applicant's FAR, lot coverage, and setback calculations treat the lots as a single combined parcel.

## Formal Requests to the Commission

We respectfully request that the Commission:

<Warning>
  **Priority Requests — Boundary and Lot Status**

  1. **Deny or continue to defer** the COA applications until the boundary dispute is resolved through an independent RPLS survey that accounts for all found control monuments.
  2. **Require the applicant to produce a recorded Replat or Agreement to Bind Properties**, or evaluate each lot independently for compliance — including per-lot FAR calculations.
  3. **Require the applicant to reconcile the fluctuating southern property line** and explain why the plans show the rear of 509 W. 15th St. on the applicant's property.
</Warning>

4. **Require the applicant to demonstrate compliance with the 15-foot cumulative side setback minimum** for two-story houses — the current drawings show only ~12 feet.

5. **Require the applicant to retain the existing driveway** as required by the Houston Heights Design Guidelines.

6. **Require the applicant to reconcile all internal inconsistencies** and reclassify the garage as attached, with corrected FAR calculations.

7. **Verify compliance with the 5' minimum side setback** on all sides, measured from the true, monumented property lines.

8. **Investigate the street pin embedded in the roadway** at the historic retaining wall location as independent boundary evidence.

---

The enclosed report provides the full analysis, including citations to the architectural plans, recorded surveys, and applicable legal authority. We have also enclosed a set of annotated exhibits showing the relevant site plan and floor plan details.

Thank you for your consideration. We are available to answer any questions and to provide additional documentation upon request.

Respectfully,

**Anthony Leavitt & LEAVITTHAAS LLC**
On behalf of the property owners at 1501 Tulane Street
