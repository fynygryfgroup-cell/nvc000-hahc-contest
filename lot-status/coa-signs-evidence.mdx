One of the most telling pieces of evidence in this record is not a survey, a floor plan, or a legal brief — it is two public notice signs posted on the property at 1505 Tulane Street. By filing two separate COA applications and posting two separate signs, the process itself has recorded an implicit acknowledgment that the HAHC recognizes these as two distinct lots with two distinct structures. That recognition is in direct tension with the applicant's insistence on combined FAR, lot coverage, and setback calculations across an un-replatted parcel.

## Section III.F — Public Notice Signs Posted on the Property: Two Signs Indicate HAHC Recognizes Separate Lots

The property currently has two signs posted on it — one for each COA application (HP2026-0244 for the garage and HP2026-0248 for the main house addition). The Houston Heights Historic District rules require public notice signage for COA applications.

The presence of two separate COA signs is itself evidence that the HAHC recognizes these as two separate lots. Each COA application corresponds to a structure on a different lot — the main house on one lot and the garage on the other. If the HAHC truly considered Lots 16 and 17 to be a single combined parcel, a single COA application covering the entire project would have been sufficient. The fact that two separate COA applications were filed, each with its own sign, strongly suggests that the HAHC's own processing system treats these as distinct lots with distinct structures.

<Warning>
**Internal Contradiction:** The HAHC's COA process appears to recognize two separate lots — two applications, two signs — yet the applicant's FAR, lot coverage, and setback calculations treat the lots as a single combined 8,799 sq ft parcel. The HAHC should reconcile this contradiction before approving either application — either by confirming the lots are separate (in which case per-lot compliance is required) or by requiring a recorded replat before proceeding.
</Warning>

The HAHC should also verify that all public notice requirements have been properly satisfied and that the signage complies with the Historic District's notification rules.

## Section VIII.C — Administrative Filing Verification

The following public records should be checked to confirm the status of any lot combination. These three steps constitute the minimum administrative verification required before the HAHC can accept the applicant's combined-lot calculations as valid:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Harris County Clerk Real Property Portal">
    Search under the owner's name for **AGR (Agreement)** or **MAP/PLAT (Replat)** filings. An Agreement to Bind Properties (unity-of-title covenant) would appear as an AGR instrument. A recorded replat would appear as a MAP or PLAT filing. If neither exists, the lots are legally separate and per-lot compliance is mandatory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Houston Plat Tracker">
    Search by address for active **Subdivision Plat/Replat applications**. Even if a replat is in process but not yet recorded, it is not legally effective. Only a fully recorded, approved replat satisfies Chapter 42's requirements. A pending application does not authorize combined-lot calculations in a COA submittal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="City of Houston Permitting Center Portal">
    Review permit notes for **structural plan review comments** regarding missing binding affidavits. If a plan reviewer has already flagged the absence of a binding agreement or replat, that flag should be visible in the permit history and should be reported to the HAHC as part of its review of these COA applications.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why This Evidence Matters

The two-sign configuration is not a minor procedural detail. It is the HAHC's own intake and public notice process expressing — through its ordinary administrative operation — that HP2026-0244 and HP2026-0248 are separate projects on separate lots. That administrative reality cannot be set aside when evaluating compliance claims that depend on treating those same lots as one.

<Warning>
The HAHC should not approve COA applications that rely on combined-lot calculations unless and until:

1. A recorded Replat or Agreement to Bind Properties is produced from the Harris County Clerk's records, **or**
2. The applicant submits per-lot compliance calculations demonstrating that **each lot independently** satisfies FAR, lot coverage, open space, and setback requirements under Chapter 42 and Chapter 33.

Absent one of these two demonstrations, approval of either application on a combined-lot basis would be inconsistent with the legal status of the property as reflected in the public record — and inconsistent with the HAHC's own two-application processing of this project.
</Warning>
