## Section III.E — The Lot Status Question: Are These One Lot or Two?

This is the threshold issue that no one has been able to answer definitively: have Lots 16 and 17 been officially joined into a single lot, or do they remain two separate lots?

The COA submittals reference "Lots 16 & 17, Block 145" [1][2] and calculate all FAR, lot coverage, and setbacks using the combined 8,799 sq ft area. However, as confirmed through public records research, no formal subdivision replat map and no Agreement to Bind Properties has been legally recorded for 1505 Tulane St. This means:

<Info>
**Status of Record:** As of the date of this report (September 24, 2026), neither a recorded Replat nor an Agreement to Bind Properties is on file with the Harris County Clerk or the City of Houston Planning Commission. The lots remain legally separate under the public record.
</Info>

### If the Lots Remain Legally Separate (the Current State of the Record)

- The house sits on one lot (the north lot) and the garage sits on the other (the south lot).
- Each lot must independently satisfy its own FAR, building coverage, open space, and setback requirements under Chapter 42 and Chapter 33.
- The proposed 3,800+ sq ft combined footprint is only permissible because it relies on the total area of both lots combined. If evaluated independently, the parcel with the actual house would be automatically disqualified — its FAR and lot coverage would far exceed what is permissible on a single ~4,400 sq ft lot.
- The garage, sitting on a separate lot without a main residence, would be an accessory structure to a non-existent main residence on that lot — an impermissible configuration.

### If the Lots Have Been Joined (Not Yet Recorded)

- The applicant must produce a recorded Replat or Agreement to Bind Properties before the COA can be approved or building permits issued.
- Neither document is currently on record.

<Warning>
**Strategic Concern:** The applicant appears to be using both lots together strategically — to maximize allowable FAR and lot coverage across the combined 8,799 sq ft — while the lots remain legally separate. The HAHC cannot approve these applications without first determining the lot status. If the lots are separate, the project as designed is non-compliant on its face. If the lots are intended to be joined, the joining must be legally recorded before approval.
</Warning>

## Section V.A — Unapproved Lot Combination (Chapter 42 Violation)

**The core problem:** Both COA submittals calculate FAR, lot coverage, and setbacks using a combined 8,799 sq ft lot area spanning Lots 16 and 17 — two separate recorded lots [3][4]. No formal replat map and no Agreement to Bind Properties has been legally recorded for 1505 Tulane St.

Under City of Houston Chapter 42 (Subdivisions & Development) and Chapter 33 (Historic Preservation), a developer cannot calculate FAR, total lot coverage, or cumulative setbacks across multiple lots unless those lots are legally tied or merged through a recorded Replat or a recorded Agreement to Bind Properties (unity-of-title covenant).

<Info>
**Status of record:** As of the date of this report, neither document is on file with the Harris County Clerk or the City of Houston Planning Commission. The City of Houston Permitting Center cannot legally release structural building permits for a single home spanning both lots until one of these processes is completed.
</Info>

<Warning>
**The strategic concern:** The applicant appears to be using both lots together strategically (to maximize allowable FAR and lot coverage across the combined 8,799 sq ft) while the lots remain legally separate. If the lots must be evaluated independently, each lot must independently satisfy its own FAR, building coverage, open space, and setback requirements — and the proposed 3,800+ sq ft combined footprint would not be permissible on either lot alone.
</Warning>

## Section III.E-1 — South Lot Misrepresentation: House Shown Instead of Existing Garage

<Warning>
**MAJOR FINDING — South Lot Misrepresentation:** On the southern lot, the submitted plans represent a house where the existing garage currently stands. The site plans show the proposed main house addition and garage structures positioned across both lots, but the representation of the south lot is inconsistent with the physical reality of the property. The existing garage — a structure that currently exists on the south lot — is not accurately represented. Instead, the plans appear to show the house footprint extending onto the south lot in a manner that does not reflect the existing conditions.

This misrepresentation is significant because it obscures the fact that the project is building across an interior lot line between two legally separate lots. If the south lot contains (or is depicted as containing) the main house rather than an accessory garage, the applicant is effectively constructing a single building spanning two un-replatted lots — a direct violation of Chapter 42. The HAHC and Permitting Office should require the applicant to clarify exactly what structure is proposed on each lot and verify the depiction against the actual existing conditions.
</Warning>
