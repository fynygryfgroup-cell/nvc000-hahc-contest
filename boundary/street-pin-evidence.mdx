Independent of the recorded survey chain and the family's 17-year history of possession, there exists a piece of physical evidence embedded in the roadway itself: a survey pin, marked with bright orange spray paint, located precisely at the historic retaining wall line. This pin was not placed by the 1501 Tulane family — it was set in the public right-of-way by a licensed surveyor, and its location directly corroborates the family's boundary position while directly contradicting the applicant's shifted boundary claim.

## Section III.C-2 — Street Pin Evidence Corroborates the Family's Boundary Claim

A survey pin embedded in the street — marked with orange survey paint — is located at a position consistent with the historic retaining wall line, not with the applicant's shifted boundary. Photographs taken at the site show the pin clearly marked in the asphalt roadway with an orange painted triangle (see Exhibit H — Photographic Evidence).

Survey pins set in public rights-of-way are typically placed by licensed surveyors to mark property line extensions or control points. The location of this pin — at the point of the historic retaining wall rather than at the applicant's claimed line — provides independent physical evidence that:

<Info>
**Point 1 — The True Property Line Sits at the Retaining Wall**

The true property line sits at the retaining wall location, as the family has maintained for 17+ years. The survey pin is positioned precisely at the retaining wall line — not at the applicant's newly claimed position further to the south.
</Info>

<Info>
**Point 2 — The Applicant's Shifted Boundary Has No Monument**

The applicant's shifted southern boundary does not correspond to any surveyed monument. No found iron rod, no pin, and no physical marker in the public right-of-way supports the applicant's claimed line. The only physical evidence in the street corroborates the family's position.
</Info>

<Info>
**Point 3 — The Pin Is Consistent with the Recorded Chain**

The recorded monumented boundary (per the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats) is consistent with the street pin location. All three independent surveys — the Buchanan survey, the Windrose Land Services survey, and the Goodale survey — establish dimensions that align with the retaining wall line where the pin is located.
</Info>
