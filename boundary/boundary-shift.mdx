The applicant has shifted the southern property line south by an estimated 3 to 5 feet in the submittal drawings, relative to the recorded monumented position — a unilateral boundary claim unsupported by any recorded survey in the chain of title. This shift has cascading effects on every compliance metric in the COA application: lot area, setbacks, FAR, and lot coverage. Even more alarming, the southern boundary is not fixed at a single shifted position — it continues to fluctuate from sheet to sheet within the applicant's own submission, at one point producing a physical impossibility.

## Section III.C — The Applicant's Unilateral Boundary Shift

The applicant has shifted the southern boundary line south by an estimated 3 to 5 feet in the submittal drawings, relative to the recorded monumented position. This shift:

1. **Artificially expands the apparent lot area** used for FAR and lot coverage calculations.
2. **Reduces the apparent building setback** from the true property line, masking what would otherwise be a setback violation.
3. **Encroaches onto land** that the 1501 Tulane St. family has maintained and improved for over 17 years (see Section IV — Adverse Possession).

<Warning>
**Three Consequences of the Boundary Shift**

The unilateral 3–5 foot southward shift of the property line in the applicant's submittal drawings produces three simultaneous compliance failures:

- **Lot Area Manipulation** — The inflated lot area (including land the applicant does not rightfully possess) is used to calculate the FAR cap, the lot coverage maximum, and the impervious cover percentage. All of these figures are artificially favorable to the applicant.
- **Setback Masking** — By claiming a southern boundary that sits further south than the true monumented line, the applicant makes setback measurements appear more generous than they actually are. If the boundary is restored to its historic position, the southern setback collapses below the mandatory 5-foot minimum.
- **Encroachment onto 1501 Tulane** — The 3–5 feet of land the applicant has claimed belongs to the 1501 Tulane St. family, which has maintained and structurally improved this strip continuously since 2009.
</Warning>

## Section III.C-1 — The Southern Property Line Continues to Fluctuate Across the Plans

<Warning>
**MAJOR FINDING — Internally Inconsistent Boundary**

The southern property line is **not consistent** across the submitted plans. Even within the applicant's own submittal packets, the southern boundary line shifts position from sheet to sheet. In some plan drawings, the line is drawn so far south that the **rear of 509 W. 15th Street appears to be on the applicant's property** — a physical impossibility given that 509 W. 15th St. is a separately owned, separately platted property (West 54' of Lot 18, per the 1996 and 2016 Buchanan surveys).
</Warning>

This fluctuation is not a minor drafting inconsistency. It indicates that the applicant has not established a fixed, defensible southern boundary line. If the applicant's own drawings cannot agree on where the southern property line sits, the HAHC cannot rely on any setback, lot coverage, or FAR calculation that depends on that boundary. Every dimension measured from a line that "moves" between sheets is unreliable.

The fact that the plans at one point show the rear of a neighboring property (509 W. 15th St.) as being within the applicant's parcel demonstrates the degree to which the southern boundary has been manipulated. This is not a surveying disagreement between two parties — it is an internal inconsistency within the applicant's own submission that defies the recorded plat record.
