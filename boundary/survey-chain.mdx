Official plats from the sale and construction of houses in this area going back to 1996 consistently establish the same boundary dimensions. Three recorded surveys — spanning over a decade of independent licensed work — agree precisely on the location of the shared southern boundary between 1505 Tulane Street and 1501 Tulane Street. The applicant's newly commissioned survey is the sole departure from this unbroken chain, and the circumstances under which it was produced raise serious questions about its reliability and objectivity.

## Section III.A — Recorded Historical Surveys

The property dimensions in this area have been consistently surveyed and recorded for decades. Three recorded boundary plats establish fixed lot dimensions that directly contradict the applicant's site plan:

The 2007 Goodale survey marks the south boundary of 1505 Tulane St. (adjacent to Lot 18 / 1501 Tulane) as 132.00' WEST, controlled by found ½" iron rods (FND ½" IR). Handwritten annotations on the 2001 plat note a callout discrepancy of 36" (3.0') versus 36.11' along the shared line.

## Section III.B — The Texas Law of Monuments

Under Texas boundary law, physical monuments set by licensed surveyors (the ½" and ⅝" iron rods documented across all three surveys) control over arbitrary boundary claims. The applicant cannot unilaterally redefine a recorded boundary on a COA submittal without providing a newly recorded boundary survey signed by a Registered Professional Land Surveyor (RPLS) that explicitly accounts for all found control monuments.

<Warning>
**Outlier Survey — Chain of Title Red Flag**

The new owner's survey is the **only plat in the entire chain** that departs from the established dimensions. Unlike the 1996, 2000/2001, and 2007 surveys — each conducted by independent licensed professionals at arm's-length transactions — the new owner's survey was commissioned after the purchase, by the new homeowner's own survey team. Under the Texas Law of Monuments, a survey that ignores or contradicts found control monuments (the ½" and ⅝" iron rods documented across the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats) **cannot override the established boundary.** The applicant's new survey is an outlier that contradicts decades of recorded plats and found monuments.
</Warning>
