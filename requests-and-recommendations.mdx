## VIII. REQUESTS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

### A. HAHC Actions

The Houston Archaeological and Historical Commission is respectfully requested to take the following actions:

1. **Deny or continue to defer COA applications HP2026-0244 and HP2026-0248 until the boundary dispute is resolved.**

   Require the applicant to submit a boundary survey signed by a Registered Professional Land Surveyor (RPLS) that accounts for all found control monuments (FND IR) documented in the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats, and that is agreed upon by adjacent property owners. The survey must reconcile the fluctuating southern property line and explain why the plans at one point show the rear of 509 W. 15th St. on the applicant's property.

2. **Require the applicant to reconcile all internal inconsistencies in the submittal packets** — including existing home square footage, ridge height, lot coverage arithmetic, window/door schedules, and the fluctuating southern boundary — before the applications are reconsidered.

3. **Verify whether a recorded Replat or Agreement to Bind Properties exists for Lots 16 and 17.**

   If not, require the applicant to complete one before the COA can be approved, and evaluate each lot independently for compliance in the interim.

4. **Require the applicant to demonstrate compliance with the cumulative side setback rule** — 15 feet minimum for two-story houses under the Houston Heights Design Guidelines.

   The current drawings show approximately 12 feet cumulative, a face-of-drawing violation.

5. **Require the applicant to retain the existing concrete/asphalt driveway** as required by the Houston Heights Design Guidelines, or demonstrate why retention is infeasible.

6. **Investigate the street pin evidence** — a survey pin embedded in the roadway, marked with orange survey paint, located at the historic retaining wall line.

   This physical evidence should be weighed alongside the recorded plat chain in determining the true boundary.

<Warning>
**Face-of-Drawing Violation — Cumulative Side Setback**

The submitted site plan drawings show a north (plat "East") side setback of 3′–6½″ and a south (plat "West") side setback of 8′–5 15/16″, yielding a cumulative total of approximately **12 feet**. The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require a minimum cumulative side setback of **15 feet** for two-story houses. This is a 3-foot deficiency that appears on the face of the applicant's own submitted drawings — independent of the boundary dispute.
</Warning>

### B. Permitting Office Actions

The City of Houston Permitting Office is respectfully requested to take the following actions:

1. **Flag the un-replatted interior lot line during plan review** and withhold structural building permits until either a Replat or Agreement to Bind Properties is recorded.

2. **Require fire code compliance verification** for any wall within 5′ of the true (monumented) property line.

3. **Audit the FAR and lot coverage calculations** against the actual floor plan dimensions — both on a combined-lot basis and on a per-lot basis (which the current lot status requires).

4. **Verify that the south lot has a permitted main residence** before approving the garage and garage apartment as accessory structures. An accessory structure without a main residence on the same lot is impermissible.

## IX. CONCLUSION

The COA applications for 1505 Tulane St. rely on mathematically flawed lot coverage metrics, unverified boundary baselines, an unapproved lot combination, and undisclosed conversion of accessory space into enclosed living area. The proposed footprint encroaches upon a historical boundary line and a retaining wall that has been continuously maintained by the 1501 Tulane St. family since 2009. The southern property line continues to fluctuate across the submitted plans — at one point showing the rear of a neighboring property (509 W. 15th St.) on the applicant's parcel. A survey pin embedded in the street corroborates the family's boundary claim at the historic retaining wall location.

The project violates the Houston Heights Design Guidelines on multiple fronts: the cumulative side setback of approximately 12 feet falls 3 feet below the 15-foot minimum for two-story houses; the existing driveway is not retained as required; and the context area misrepresents camelbacks as full two-story buildings to justify incompatible massing. When evaluated on a per-lot basis (as required without a recorded replat), the north lot's FAR far exceeds the 40% maximum, and the south lot's garage is an impermissible accessory structure without a main residence.

Until the boundary dispute is resolved through an independent RPLS survey, the lot combination is legally formalized, and the internal inconsistencies in the submittal packets are corrected, the Commission cannot reliably determine whether the project complies with the Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines or the City of Houston Code of Ordinances. The authors respectfully request that the applications be denied or deferred pending resolution of these issues.
