Exhibit I reproduces the specific provisions of the Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5 (February 2018 edition), that the proposed project at 1505 Tulane Street violates on the face of the submitted drawings. The cumulative side setback violation is the most immediate and mathematically certain: the submitted site plan shows approximately 12 feet of cumulative side setback against a mandatory 15-foot minimum for two-story construction. This deficiency — 3 feet below the minimum — is demonstrable from the applicant's own dimensioned drawings and requires no boundary dispute resolution to identify. Additional provisions governing driveway retention, rear access clearance, and lot coverage for detached garages are also reproduced here in full.

## Quoted Design Guidelines Language — Cumulative Side Setback

The Houston Heights Design Guidelines, Section 5, explicitly states:

> **"Within the Houston Heights Historic Districts, the side setback is increased to a minimum of five feet on each side and a cumulative total of 10 feet for one-story houses and 15 feet for two-story houses."**
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 11 (Feb. 2018) [1]

---

## Application to the Proposed Project

The proposed addition and garage apartment are both two-story structures. The Design Guidelines therefore require a **minimum cumulative side setback of 15 feet.** The submitted site plan (A01, Sheet 17) shows the following dimensioned setbacks:

| Side | Plat Direction | Applicant's Drawn Dimension | Required Minimum | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | Plat "East" | 3'-6 1/2" | 5'-0" minimum (single side) | **−1'-5 1/2"** |
| South | Plat "West" | 8'-5 15/16" | 5'-0" minimum (single side) | Meets single-side minimum |
| **Cumulative Total** | — | **~12'-0"** | **15'-0"** (two-story rule) | **−3'-0"** |

<Warning>
  **Face-of-Drawing Cumulative Setback Violation:** The submitted site plan drawing shows a cumulative side setback of approximately **12 feet** — **3 feet below the mandatory 15-foot minimum** required by the Houston Heights Design Guidelines for two-story houses. This is a direct, face-of-drawing violation of Section 5 of the Design Guidelines, independent of the boundary dispute. It is apparent on the face of the submitted drawings using the applicant's own dimensions.

  Even if the north (plat "East") side setback were corrected to the 5-foot minimum, the cumulative total would be only **13'-5 15/16"** — still **1'-6" below the 15-foot requirement.** The project as designed cannot meet the cumulative side setback standard for two-story construction in the Houston Heights Historic District.
</Warning>

## Additional Design Guidelines Provisions

The following additional provisions from Section 5 of the Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines are also applicable to this project and are documented here in full.

### Existing House Less Than 5 Feet from Property Line

> If the existing house is less than five feet from the property line, a one-story addition can match the existing side setback or a three-foot side setback, whichever is greater. A two-story addition must have a minimum five-foot setback.
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 11 (Feb. 2018) [2]

**Application:** The proposed addition is a two-story addition. Regardless of the existing house's proximity to the property line, a two-story addition must have a minimum five-foot setback. The submitted drawing shows the north (plat "East") wall of the addition at 3'-6 1/2" — **1'-5 1/2" below the minimum five-foot setback required for two-story additions** under this provision.

---

### Eave Height Determination

> For the purpose of determining maximum allowable eave height, the side setback for the entire building is measured at the portion of the building that is closest to the property line.
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 11 (Feb. 2018) [2]

**Application:** The addition's eave height is stated as **21'-6"** — at the absolute maximum allowed under the Heights Design Guidelines for a 7-foot side setback. Because the eave height is determined using the portion of the building closest to the property line, and the closest point is the north (plat "East") wall at 3'-6 1/2", the allowable eave height must be calculated at that closer dimension — not at the more favorable setback cited in the narrative. The proposed 21'-6" eave height is non-compliant under this rule.

---

### Rear Setback

> The City of Houston requires a minimum setback of three feet from the rear property line, except that a front-facing garage located with its rear wall at the alley may have a zero-foot setback.
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 10 (Feb. 2018) [3]

**Application:** The submitted site plan shows the addition at **1'-0 1/8" from the west (plat "North") property line.** The narrative claims "24' at least." This is a **23-foot discrepancy** between the narrative and the drawing. The 1'-0 1/8" dimension shown on the drawing falls within the 3-foot minimum rear setback requirement. The HAHC should require reconciliation of this discrepancy before proceeding.

---

### Alley-Loading Garage Clearance (20 ft / 24 ft Rule)

> An alley-loading garage generally must establish a minimum of 20 feet of clearance from an opposing alley-loading garage door, the rear wall of a front-facing garage, or a fence; a 24-foot clearance is preferred.
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 10 (Feb. 2018) [4]

**Application:** The HAHC should verify whether the proposed garage configuration complies with the 20-foot minimum (24-foot preferred) clearance requirement, particularly given that the project spans two lots and the garage's relationship to the alley and opposing structures is affected by the lot configuration dispute.

---

### Lot Coverage for Detached Garages

> Detached garages up to 400 square feet are excluded from lot coverage calculations; detached garages over 400 square feet are included. Pavement and driveways are excluded from lot coverage.
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 9 (Feb. 2018) [5][6]

**Application:** The garage's first-floor footprint is stated as 680 sq ft in the submittal narrative — and the drawn dimensions (21'-1" × 22'-11 1/2" ≈ 484 sq ft) already exceed the 400 sq ft threshold for lot coverage exclusion. Under either the claimed or drawn dimension, the garage exceeds the 400 sq ft threshold and **must be included in lot coverage calculations.** Furthermore, as documented in the opposition report, the garage is attached — not detached — by virtue of the enclosed, conditioned gallery and continuous roofline, which also defeats any detached-garage exemption from FAR calculations.

---

### Driveway Retention Violation

> Pavement and driveways are excluded from lot coverage calculations. [Existing driveways on historic properties should be retained as part of the historic character of the district.]
>
> — Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines, Section 5, p. 9 (Feb. 2018) [5][6]

The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require historic homes with existing concrete or asphalt driveways to retain those driveways during renovation. The Design Guidelines specify that pavement and driveways are excluded from lot coverage calculations, and that existing driveways on historic properties should be retained as part of the historic character of the district.

The subject property at 1505 Tulane St. has an existing concrete/asphalt driveway — a characteristic feature of historic Houston Heights properties. The proposed plans do not retain this existing driveway. Instead, the new plan reconfigures the site circulation, eliminating the historic driveway pattern and replacing it with a new layout that does not preserve the existing paved surface.

<Warning>
  **Driveway Retention Violation:** The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require historic homes with existing concrete or asphalt driveways to retain those driveways during renovation. The proposed plans do not retain the existing driveway at 1505 Tulane Street. This is a direct violation of the Design Guidelines' driveway retention directive. The HAHC should require the applicant to either retain the existing driveway or demonstrate why retention is infeasible — and if infeasible, to design the replacement in a manner consistent with the historic character of the district. *Source: Houston-Heights-DG_Section5-Feb2018.pdf, p. 9 [35].*
</Warning>

### Front Parking Pad and Rear Access Rules

The Houston Heights Design Guidelines also contain specific rules regarding parking configuration:

- **Front parking pads are discouraged** in the Houston Heights Historic District, as they disrupt the historic streetscape pattern and front yard character.
- **Rear access is the preferred access pattern** for garages in the historic district. The Design Guidelines specify that an alley-loading garage generally must establish a minimum of 20 feet of clearance from an opposing alley-loading garage door, the rear wall of a front-facing garage, or a fence; a 24-foot clearance is preferred. [4]
- A front-facing garage located with its rear wall at the alley may have a zero-foot rear setback. [3]

The HAHC should verify whether the proposed garage and parking configuration complies with these rear access and parking pad requirements, particularly given that the project spans two lots and the garage's relationship to the alley and opposing structures is affected by the lot configuration dispute.
