Exhibit H compiles the photographic record that provides independent, on-the-ground corroboration of the 1501 Tulane Street family's boundary claim. The photographs document three categories of physical evidence: the survey pin embedded in the asphalt roadway and marked with orange survey paint; the 2009 retaining wall, its concrete footings, and the landscaped side yard maintained by the family for over seventeen years; and the spatial relationship between the two structures at the disputed boundary line. Taken together, this photographic record substantiates the boundary position established by the recorded 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats — and directly contradicts the applicant's shifted southern boundary.

## Significance of the Street Pin Evidence

<Info>
  The street pin provides independent physical evidence that the true property line sits at the retaining wall location. This evidence carries three distinct evidentiary weights:

  **1. The street pin corroborates the recorded monumented boundary.** Survey pins set in public rights-of-way are placed by licensed surveyors to mark property line extensions or control points. The location of this pin — at the point of the historic retaining wall rather than at the applicant's claimed line — is consistent with the recorded boundary dimensions per the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats. It provides independent physical evidence that the true property line sits at the retaining wall location, as the family has maintained for 17+ years.

  **2. The street pin contradicts the applicant's shifted southern boundary.** The applicant's shifted southern boundary does not correspond to any surveyed monument visible in the public right-of-way. A survey pin embedded in the street at the retaining wall location directly contradicts the claim that the property line sits 3–5 feet further south.

  **3. The street pin supports the adverse possession and boundary-by-acquiescence claim.** The physical evidence of a licensed surveyor's pin placed at the retaining wall location — combined with the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plat chain and the 17+ years of continuous family maintenance and structural possession — supports both the adverse possession claim under Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code § 16.026 and the boundary-by-acquiescence doctrine established in Gulf Oil Corp. v. Marathon Oil Co., 152 S.W.2d 711 (Tex. 1941). The pin demonstrates that the applicant's boundary shift is not supported by any surveyed monument.
</Info>
