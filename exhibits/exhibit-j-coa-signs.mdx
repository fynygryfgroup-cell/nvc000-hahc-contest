The contradiction works as follows:

**What two signs imply — two separate lots:**
- If the HAHC truly considered Lots 16 and 17 to be a single combined parcel, a single COA application covering the entire project would have been sufficient.

## Significance of the Two-Sign Evidence

If the HAHC truly considered Lots 16 and 17 to be a single combined parcel, a single COA application covering the entire project would have been sufficient. The fact that two separate COA applications were filed, each with its own sign, strongly suggests the HAHC's own processing system treats these as distinct lots with distinct structures.

This creates an internal contradiction: the HAHC's COA process appears to recognize two separate lots (two applications, two signs), yet the applicant's FAR, lot coverage, and setback calculations treat the lots as a single combined parcel. The HAHC should reconcile this contradiction before approving either application — either by confirming the lots are separate (in which case per-lot compliance is required) or by requiring a recorded replat before proceeding.

The HAHC should also verify that all public notice requirements have been properly satisfied and that the signage complies with the Historic District's notification rules.

---

## What Per-Lot Compliance Would Require

If the Commission determines that Lots 16 and 17 are separate lots — as the two-sign evidence suggests — the project must be evaluated on a per-lot basis. The combined 8,799 sq ft lot area splits into two approximately equal lots of **~4,400 sq ft each.**

| Lot | Structures | Per-Lot FAR Cap (40%) | Estimated Actual FAR | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North Lot (Lot 16) | Main house + two-story addition | ~1,760 sq ft max | ~2,500–3,300+ sq ft | **Far exceeds cap** |
| South Lot (Lot 17) | Garage + garage apartment | ~1,760 sq ft max | ~1,000–1,133 sq ft (numerically under cap) | **Impermissible — no main residence on lot** |

The south lot has a more fundamental problem beyond FAR: a garage and garage apartment are accessory structures, and an accessory structure cannot be the primary structure on a lot that has no main residence. Under City of Houston ordinances, an accessory structure is only permitted in conjunction with a main building on the same lot. If the garage sits on the south lot without a main residence, it is an **impermissible configuration** — regardless of whether the FAR number is within limits.

## Requested Actions

The Commission is respectfully requested to:

1. **Reconcile the internal contradiction** between the two-sign evidence (suggesting two separate lots) and the applicant's combined-lot calculations (treating both lots as one parcel).
2. **Verify the lot status** — confirm whether a recorded Replat or Agreement to Bind Properties exists for Lots 16 and 17. If not, require the applicant to complete one before either COA can be approved, and evaluate each lot independently for compliance in the interim.
3. **Verify public notice compliance** — confirm that all public notice requirements for both applications have been properly satisfied and that the signage complies with the Historic District's notification rules.
4. **Require per-lot FAR analysis** if no replat is on record — including an assessment of whether the south lot's garage is an impermissible accessory structure without a main residence.
