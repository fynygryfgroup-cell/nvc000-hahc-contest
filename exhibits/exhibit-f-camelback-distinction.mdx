Exhibit F presents the context area pages drawn from both COA submittal packets — A01, Sheet 11 and A02, Sheet 9 — and demonstrates that the applicant's labeling of neighboring properties systematically misrepresents the architectural character of the Tulane block. By collapsing the distinction between full two-story buildings and camelbacks into a single "2 STY" tag, the submittals construct a false compatibility argument for a full two-story massing proposal that is, in reality, out of character with every contributing structure on the immediate block.

## Four Key Findings

### Finding 1 — The "2 STY" Label Obscures the Camelback Distinction

The "2 STY" label obscures the camelback distinction. 1501 and 1515 Tulane are camelbacks — a specific, historically appropriate Houston Heights form consisting of a 1-story bungalow with a smaller, subordinate 2-story rear portion. The 2-story portion is set back and reduced in scale relative to the primary 1-story mass. A camelback is predominantly a 1-story building. 1511 Tulane is a true 1-story with no camelback at all. Only 1530 Ashland is a full 2-story.

<Warning>
  **Critical Mislabeling — 1511 Tulane Street:** The submittal designates 1511 Tulane as "Contributing – 2 STY." In fact, 1511 Tulane is a true 1-story structure with no camelback element whatsoever. This is a direct factual error on the face of the context area documentation submitted to the Commission.
</Warning>

---

### Finding 2 — The Applicant's Proposed Massing Is NOT a Camelback

The applicant's proposed massing is NOT a camelback. The applicant's design is a full 2-story rear addition and a full 2-story garage apartment — with a **21'-6" eave height** and a **27'-7" ridge height** extending the full width and depth of the addition. A camelback is a subordinate, smaller 2-story element behind a dominant 1-story primary form.

By labeling camelbacks as simply "2 STY," the applicant creates a false equivalence between:

- **Historic camelbacks** — predominantly 1-story with a subordinate 2-story rear bump, set back and reduced in scale
- **The proposed full 2-story massing** — a full-scale 2-story structure with a 21'-6" eave height and 27'-7" ridge height extending the full width and depth of the addition

This false equivalence makes the applicant's full 2-story design appear compatible with the context area, when in fact the context area consists of 1-story homes and camelbacks — not full 2-story buildings.

| Characteristic | Historic Camelback | Applicant's Proposed Design |
|---|---|---|
| Primary street-facing form | 1-story bungalow | Full 2-story |
| 2-story element | Subordinate, set-back rear portion | Full width and depth of addition |
| Eave height | Low, 1-story primary eave | 21'-6" |
| Ridge height | Subordinate rear ridge | 27'-7" |
| Character | Predominantly 1-story | Full 2-story |

### Finding 3 — 1530 Ashland Was Cherry-Picked from a Different Block

1530 Ashland was cherry-picked from a different block. The only genuine full 2-story in the context area is on Ashland Street — a different block from the Tulane properties. It was built before the historic board even existed, making it an improper comparable for a historic district context analysis.

> The applicant's proposed design is a full 2-story rear addition and a full 2-story garage apartment — not a camelback. A camelback is a subordinate, smaller 2-story element behind a dominant 1-story primary form. The applicant's proposed 2-story mass is full-scale, with a 21'-6" eave height and a 27'-7" ridge height, extending the full width and depth of the addition. This is fundamentally different in scale, massing, and character from a camelback.

The proper context area analysis under the Heights Design Guidelines requires that:

- "The height of new construction must not be taller than the typical height of existing contributing structures in the context area."
- "The scale and proportions of the new construction… must be compatible with the typical scale and proportions of existing contributing structures in the context area."
- "A new construction shall not be constructed with more than one story in a historic district that is comprised entirely of one-story contributing structures."

When the context area is accurately described — with 1530 Ashland properly excluded as a non-comparable from a different block — the **actual context area on the Tulane block is 1-story homes and camelbacks, not full 2-story buildings.** The proposed full 2-story massing is not compatible with this context.

---

### Finding 4 — A Full 2-Story Contributing Structure Behind the Property Was Omitted

A full 2-story contributing structure located behind 1501 Tulane St. (sharing a northern plat line with 1505 Tulane St.) has been omitted entirely from the context area documentation. The context map shows the lots directly behind 1505 Tulane (along Ashland Street, including 1504 and 1506 Ashland), but no photo, text description, or contributing/non-contributing status is provided for these properties.

This omission is significant because:

- The HAHC requires a complete and accurate context area analysis. Omitting an adjacent contributing structure deprives the Commission of information needed to evaluate compatibility.
- The selective inclusion of only 1530 Ashland (a full 2-story from a different block) while omitting a full 2-story structure that actually shares a plat line with the subject property further suggests the context area was curated to support the applicant's massing claims rather than to accurately represent the neighborhood.
- **The applicant's own photos contradict their labels.** The HAHC can verify this by looking at the context area photographs — the photos of 1501, 1511, and 1515 Tulane clearly show 1-story primary forms (with camelback rear portions visible on 1501 and 1515), not full 2-story buildings.

<Warning>
  **Face-of-Submittal Contradiction:** The applicant's own context area photographs — submitted as part of the COA packets — visually confirm 1-story primary forms and camelback rear portions at 1501 and 1515 Tulane. The photographs contradict the "2 STY" labels applied to those same properties in the adjacent data table on the same sheet. The HAHC should compare the photographs to the labels on A01 Sheet 11 and A02 Sheet 9 directly.
</Warning>

## Summary of Context Area Misrepresentation

The Tulane block, accurately described, consists of:

- **1501 Tulane** — Camelback (1-story primary form, subordinate 2-story rear) — *labeled "2 STY" by applicant*
- **1511 Tulane** — True 1-story, no camelback — *labeled "2 STY" by applicant*
- **1515 Tulane** — Camelback (1-story primary form, subordinate 2-story rear) — *labeled "2 STY" by applicant*
- **1530 Ashland** — Full 2-story, but on a different block, built before the historic board — *sole basis for applicant's full 2-story massing claim*

The applicant's full 2-story proposed massing — with a 21'-6" eave and 27'-7" ridge — is not a camelback and is not compatible with the actual character of the Tulane block as documented by the applicant's own submitted photographs.
