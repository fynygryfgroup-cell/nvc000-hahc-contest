## Key Details by Violation

<Accordion title="P-1 — Fire-Rated Wall Construction Required (IRC Table R302.1)">

The proposed site plan shows the addition's north (plat "East") side wall at **3'-6 1/2" from the north (plat "East") property line.** Under IRC Table R302.1, any exterior wall located less than 5 feet from a property line must have a **1-hour fire-resistance rating** (tested per ASTM E119/UL 263) from both sides. Walls located less than 3 feet from a property line generally permit 0% openings — no unprotected windows or doors.

The submitted elevation drawings show standard wall construction and window placements along the north (plat "East") elevation. If this wall is within 3'-6 1/2" of the property line as drawn, the drawings fail to incorporate the required fire-rated assemblies and may show prohibited openings. The Permitting Office should flag this during plan review.

This issue is compounded by the boundary dispute: if the true property line is even closer than the applicant claims, the fire separation distance shrinks further, increasing the fire-rating requirements.

</Accordion>

<Warning>
  **P-1 is a face-of-drawing violation independent of the boundary dispute.** The addition's north wall is drawn at 3'-6 1/2" from the property line on the applicant's own site plan. At that distance, IRC Table R302.1 mandates 1-hour fire-rated construction — not shown anywhere in the submittal drawings. If the true property line is restored to its monumented position, this wall may fall within the 3-foot zone, requiring 0% openings and eliminating all depicted windows on that elevation.
</Warning>

<Accordion title="P-2 — Garage Apartment Constitutes a Second Dwelling Unit (IRC R302.3)">

The garage apartment includes a **full bathroom** (tub/shower, vanity, toilet), a **kitchen** (countertop, sink, appliance spaces), and a **living/sleeping area with a closet.** This constitutes a complete, independent dwelling unit — an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) or second dwelling unit.

If the garage apartment is permitted as a separate dwelling unit (rather than a guest suite within a single-family home), building codes require:

- **1-hour fire separation** between the two dwelling units (IRC R302.3) — floor/ceiling and wall assemblies separating the main house from the garage apartment.
- **Independent egress** — each unit must have an independent, direct path of egress to the exterior without traveling through the other unit's private spaces.

The submittals do not indicate whether fire separation assemblies are specified between the dwelling units, or whether the garage apartment has independent egress. The Permitting Office should verify the occupancy classification and require appropriate fire separation.

</Accordion>

<Warning>
  **P-2 — Second Dwelling Unit:** The garage apartment as drawn constitutes a complete, independent dwelling unit. Neither 1-hour fire separation assemblies (IRC R302.3) nor independent egress are addressed anywhere in the submittal packets. This is a life safety deficiency that cannot be waived at the HAHC level — it requires Permitting Office review and resolution before any building permit can issue.
</Warning>

<Accordion title="P-3 — House Raising Permit and Structural Engineering">

The narrative states the existing house will be raised to a **32" finished floor height**, with the four corners of the historic footprint maintained. Raising an existing structure requires:

- A **House Moving / House Leveling Permit** from the City of Houston.
- **Structural engineering drawings** for new reinforced foundation piers, crawlspace framing, tie-downs, and windstorm/flood compliance.
- **Inspection** of the existing structure's integrity before and after raising.

The COA submittal packets are architectural design documents, not structural engineering documents. The Permitting Office should verify that structural engineering plans for the house raising have been submitted and reviewed.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="P-4 — Garage Fire Separation Requirements (IRC R302.6)">

The habitable second-floor living space directly above the garage requires a minimum of **5/8" Type X gypsum board** on the garage ceiling. Walls separating the garage from the first-floor conditioned living space require minimum **1/2" gypsum board.** Any door connecting the garage to the interior must be **solid wood (≥1-3/8"), solid or honeycomb core steel, or have a 20-minute fire rating**, equipped with a self-closing device.

The submittal drawings do not appear to specify these required fire-rated assemblies. The Permitting Office should verify compliance during plan review.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="P-5 — Egress from Garage Apartment (IRC Section R310)">

Occupants of the garage apartment's second floor must currently travel through the enclosed gallery connector and use the main house's central stairway to reach the ground level. The Permitting Office should verify that the garage apartment has code-compliant **emergency escape and rescue openings (EERO)** opening directly to the exterior, meeting IRC Section R310 minimums:

- Minimum **5.7 sq ft net clear opening**
- Minimum **24" height**
- Minimum **20" width**
- Maximum **44" sill height**

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="P-6 — Impervious Cover Calculation May Be Affected by Boundary Dispute">

The submittal calculates total impervious coverage at **4,453 sq ft (50.61%)** against a 70% maximum on the 8,799 sq ft lot. However, if the boundary dispute reduces the actual lot area — by moving the southern line north to its monumented position — the lot area decreases and the impervious cover percentage increases. The Permitting Office should verify the impervious cover calculation against the true, surveyed lot area.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="P-7 — Missing Foundation and Structural Plans">

The COA submittal packets do not include dedicated foundation plans, structural engineering drawings, or structural calculations. For a project of this scope — raising an existing historic house, building a two-story addition, and constructing a two-story garage with an apartment — the Permitting Office requires:

- **Foundation plans** showing slab/footing/pier details
- **Structural calculations** for the elevated house, new addition, and garage
- **Windstorm and flood compliance documentation**

The absence of these documents from the COA packets does not necessarily mean they haven't been submitted to the Permitting Office separately, but it does mean the HAHC and the public have not had the opportunity to review whether the structural design is feasible and whether it accounts for the boundary conditions.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="P-8 — Elevator Installation Permit (ASME A17.1/A18.1)">

The plans show a **residential elevator (5'-0" × 4'-0")** connecting the first and second floors through the gallery. Elevator installation requires:

- A **separate elevator permit** from the City of Houston.
- Compliance with **ASME A17.1 / A18.1 standards.**
- **Inspection and certification** before operation.

The Permitting Office should verify that the elevator permit application has been submitted.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="P-9 — Soil Drilling Without a Permit (Chapter 40, Article XII)">

**NEW FINDING** — Soil borings appear to have been conducted at 1505 Tulane St. without the required permits. A search of the City of Houston's Online Sold Permit database reveals that **no building permits have been pulled** for 1505 Tulane or 1505 1/2 Tulane — under any address variation or under the owner's name (Paul Griffin). The database covers the last approximately 3 years (2023–2026), and no permits appear under "1505 TULANE," "1505 1/2 TULANE," or "PAUL GRIFFIN" in any field (job address, buyer's name, applicant name, or owner-occupant).

If soil borings or geotechnical drilling has been performed on the property — as appears to be the case based on site observations — the following permit requirements apply:

- **Monitoring Well/Boring Permit (Chapter 40, Article XII):** If any boring was conducted in the public right-of-way (street, sidewalk, or easement), a Monitoring Well/Boring Permit from the City Engineer is required. This permit requires an application, a GIMS utility map, insurance certificates, a surety bond ($1,000 for the first facility, $750 each additional), and a fee.
- **Geotechnical Consultant Responsibilities:** The City of Houston's Geotechnical and Environmental Requirements place the burden on the Geotechnical Consultant to "ensure necessary permits are obtained before commencement of drilling" and to use the property owner's help to locate underground utilities before drilling begins. Drilling cannot start until utility lines are cleared and marked.
- **Texas 811 Utility Notification:** At least 48 hours before any drilling, Texas 811 must be called to mark underground utilities. Soil borings of even 4–6 feet can strike utility lines.
- **Trespass Concern:** If any soil sampling was conducted on the adjacent 1501 Tulane St. property or on the disputed boundary strip without the property owner's written permission, it constitutes **criminal trespass under Texas Penal Code § 30.05.** Texas courts have specifically held that conducting geophysical operations or soil sampling on someone's land is a "valuable property right which belongs exclusively to the owner."

The absence of any permit record in the City's database suggests that if soil drilling has occurred, it may have been done without the required permits. The Permitting Office should investigate whether geotechnical work has been performed on the site without proper authorization, and whether any such work encroached onto the neighboring 1501 Tulane St. property.

</Accordion>

<Warning>
  **P-9 — Unpermitted Drilling and Potential Criminal Trespass:** No permits of any kind appear in the City of Houston Online Sold Permit database for 1505 Tulane or 1505 1/2 Tulane under any address variation or the owner's name. If soil borings were conducted on or near the disputed boundary strip without the written permission of the 1501 Tulane St. property owners, this potentially constitutes criminal trespass under Texas Penal Code § 30.05. The Permitting Office is requested to investigate.
</Warning>

## Summary

These nine permitting violations are independent of the HAHC design review issues and must be resolved through the City of Houston Permitting Office's plan review process. The HAHC is encouraged to flag these items for referral to the Permitting Center and to note that no building permits should be released for this project until all life safety and structural deficiencies have been reviewed and addressed.
