Exhibit D documents the chain of recorded boundary surveys that establish the property dimensions governing the shared boundary between 1505 and 1501 Tulane Street. Every official plat in this chain — spanning three decades from 1996 through 2016 — consistently establishes the same boundary dimensions. The applicant's new survey, commissioned after the purchase and reportedly requiring a second site visit after the owner was dissatisfied with the initial results, is the single outlier in an otherwise unbroken record of consistent monumented measurements.

<Warning>
**Outlier Survey — One Against Many:** The applicant's new post-purchase survey is the **only plat in the entire 30-year chain** that departs from the established boundary dimensions. Every other recorded survey — 1996, 2000/2001, and 2007 — agrees on the same dimensions. Under the **Texas Law of Monuments**, a survey that ignores or contradicts found control monuments (the ½" and ⅝" iron rods documented across the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats) cannot override the established boundary. The applicant's survey is an outlier that contradicts decades of recorded plats and found monuments.
</Warning>

## Surveyor Sent Back Twice

The new owner's survey is not merely a statistical outlier — the circumstances of its production are independently concerning. According to the facts available, the survey team commissioned by the new homeowner after the February/March 2026 purchase had to **return to the property a second time** after the new homeowner was dissatisfied with their initial results.

This pattern — a surveyor being sent back because the owner "didn't like the results" — raises serious questions about the objectivity and reliability of the final survey product. A licensed Registered Professional Land Surveyor (RPLS) has statutory and ethical obligations to report boundary conditions accurately and in accordance with found control monuments, regardless of client preferences. A survey revised to produce a different boundary result following owner dissatisfaction does not meet the standard of an objective, legally defensible boundary determination.

## The Texas Law of Monuments

Under Texas boundary law, **physical monuments set by licensed surveyors** — the ½" and ⅝" iron rods documented across all three consistent surveys — **control over arbitrary boundary claims**. The applicant cannot unilaterally redefine a recorded boundary on a COA submittal without providing a newly recorded boundary survey signed by a Registered Professional Land Surveyor (RPLS) that explicitly accounts for all found control monuments.

| Principle | Application to This Case |
|---|---|
| Physical monuments (iron rods) control over courses and distances | The ½" and ⅝" iron rods found and documented across the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats establish the true boundary |
| A survey that ignores found control monuments cannot override established boundaries | The applicant's post-purchase survey, if it departs from the established monument chain, is legally insufficient to redefine the boundary |
| A new boundary survey must account for ALL found control monuments | Any RPLS survey submitted to the HAHC must document and reconcile every found iron rod in the chain |

## Fluctuating Southern Property Line — Internal Inconsistency

Beyond the outlier status of the applicant's new survey relative to the historical chain, the submitted plans contain a **major internal inconsistency**: the southern property line is not even consistent across the applicant's own submitted plan sheets. Even within the applicant's own submittal packets, the southern boundary line shifts position from sheet to sheet. In some plan drawings, the line is drawn so far south that **the rear of 509 W. 15th Street appears to be on the applicant's property** — a physical impossibility given that 509 W. 15th St. is a separately owned, separately platted property (West 54' of Lot 18, per the 1996 and 2016 Buchanan surveys).

This fluctuation is not a minor drafting inconsistency. It indicates that the applicant has not established a fixed, defensible southern boundary line. If the applicant's own drawings cannot agree on where the southern property line sits, the HAHC cannot rely on any setback, lot coverage, or FAR calculation that depends on that boundary. Every dimension measured from a line that "moves" between sheets is unreliable.

## Street Pin Evidence — Independent Corroboration

A survey pin embedded in the street — marked with orange survey paint — is located at a position consistent with the historic retaining wall line, not with the applicant's shifted boundary. Photographs taken at the site show the pin clearly marked in the asphalt roadway with an orange painted triangle.

Survey pins set in public rights-of-way are typically placed by licensed surveyors to mark property line extensions or control points. The location of this pin — at the point of the historic retaining wall rather than at the applicant's claimed line — provides independent physical evidence that:

1. The true property line sits at the retaining wall location, as the family has maintained for 17+ years.
2. The applicant's shifted southern boundary does not correspond to any surveyed monument.
3. The recorded monumented boundary (per the 1996, 2001, and 2007 plats) is consistent with the street pin location.

## Boundary Shift — Impact on COA Calculations

The applicant has shifted the southern boundary line south by an estimated **3 to 5 feet** in the submittal drawings, relative to the recorded monumented position. This shift:

| Effect | Description |
|---|---|
| **Artificially expands lot area** | FAR and lot coverage calculations are inflated by the additional square footage claimed through the shifted boundary |
| **Reduces apparent setback** | The shifted boundary masks what would otherwise be a setback violation on the southern (plat "West") side |
| **Encroaches on possessed land** | The shifted line encroaches onto land that the 1501 Tulane St. family has maintained and improved for over 17 years |
| **Destabilizes all calculations** | Because every setback, FAR, and coverage figure is measured from property lines, a fluctuating boundary makes all submitted calculations unverifiable |

<Info>
The HAHC cannot reliably determine compliance with the Houston Heights Design Guidelines or City of Houston Code of Ordinances using setback, FAR, or lot coverage figures measured from a property line that fluctuates between plan sheets and contradicts the recorded monumented boundary chain.
</Info>
