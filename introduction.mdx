This executive summary consolidates the material deficiencies, inconsistencies, and code violations identified across the two Certificate of Appropriateness applications submitted for 1505 Tulane Street in the Houston Heights West Historic District. It is drawn directly from the formal opposition report prepared by Anthony Leavitt & LEAVITTHAAS LLC on behalf of the adjacent property owners at 1501 Tulane Street, dated September 24, 2026. The summary reflects findings verified against the actual architectural plans and recorded surveys, and is intended to give the Commission and Permitting Office a clear, structured account of why both applications should be denied or continued to be deferred.

## I. Executive Summary

This report documents material deficiencies, inconsistencies, and code violations in the two Certificate of Appropriateness (COA) applications submitted for 1505 Tulane Street in the Houston Heights West Historic District:

- **HP2026-0248** — Main House Addition & Rehabilitation (Architectural Set A01)
- **HP2026-0244** — Detached Garage with Second-Floor Apartment (Architectural Set A02)

At the September 10, 2026 hearing, the HAHC deferred these applications after the adjacent property owner at 1501 Tulane St. raised a property line dispute affecting the southern boundary of 1505 Tulane St. This report consolidates the full factual and regulatory record supporting a request that the Commission deny or continue to defer these applications until the following are resolved:

<Warning>
  **Eight Critical Grounds for Denial or Deferral**

  The following findings represent material, independently verifiable deficiencies in the COA submittals. Several of these violations are apparent on the face of the submitted drawings — they do not depend on the resolution of the boundary dispute.

  1. **Active boundary dispute on the southern property line** — All setback, lot coverage, and FAR calculations are unverified. The southern property line continues to fluctuate across the submitted plans — at one point showing the rear of 509 W. 15th Street as being on the applicant's property.

  2. **Unapproved lot combination** — Lots 16 and 17 are being treated as a single parcel without a recorded replat or Agreement to Bind Properties, in violation of City of Houston Chapter 42. The project proposes an illegal build across the un-replatted interior lot line, with the main house on one lot and the garage on the other.

  3. **Internal inconsistencies and arithmetic errors** — The submittal packets contain contradictory figures for existing home square footage (2,192 vs. 1,299 sq ft), a ridge height discrepancy, a lot coverage arithmetic error, and a 196 sq ft discrepancy between the drawn garage footprint (~484 sq ft) and the claimed footprint (680 sq ft). These call into question the accuracy of all FAR, lot coverage, and dimensional compliance claims.

  4. **Potential fire code violations** — Construction within disputed setback buffers triggers mandatory 1-hour fire-rated construction requirements that are not reflected in the submitted elevation drawings.

  5. **Cumulative side setback violation** — The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require 15 feet of cumulative side setback for two-story houses. The submitted drawings show approximately 12 feet cumulative — a direct, face-of-drawing violation that does not depend on the boundary dispute.

  6. **Per-lot FAR violations** — When Lots 16 and 17 are evaluated independently (as they must be without a recorded replat), the north lot's main house FAR far exceeds the 40% maximum permitted on a single ~4,400 sq ft lot.

  7. **Driveway retention violation** — The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require historic homes with existing concrete or asphalt driveways to retain those driveways during renovation. The new plan does not.

  8. **Street pin evidence** — A survey pin embedded in the street, marked with orange survey paint, indicates the property line at the location of the historic retaining wall, corroborating the family's boundary claim.
</Warning>

## II. Property Description and Parties

### Note on Directions

The plat drawings for 1505 Tulane St. use a rotated orientation that does not match real-world cardinal directions. Throughout this report, directions are given as **real-world direction (plat "X")** — for example, "the north (plat 'East') side setback" refers to the real-world north side of the property, which is labeled "East" on the plat drawings. The mapping is:

| Plat Label | Real-World Direction | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Plat "East" | **North** | Neighbor side |
| Plat "South" | **East** | Tulane Street frontage |
| Plat "West" | **South** | 1501 Tulane St. / boundary dispute side |
| Plat "North" | **West** | Rear / building area |

### A. The Subject Property — 1505 Tulane Street

1505 Tulane St. is identified on both COA submittals as **Lots 16 & 17, Block 145, Houston Heights**. The applications describe it as a 2,192 square foot one-story wood single-family residence situated on an **8,799 square foot (66'66" × 132') corner lot**.

<Warning>
  **Critical Observation:** The legal description references two separate lots (Lots 16 and 17). As documented in the full report, no replat or binding agreement has been recorded to legally merge these lots into a single parcel. This is central to the compliance analysis.
</Warning>

### B. The Adjacent Property — 1501 Tulane Street

1501 Tulane St. is the home of the authors' parents, located immediately to the south of 1505 Tulane St. The family has owned and occupied this property continuously since 2009; the authors' sister and her wife owned it from 2000 to 2009 — **a combined family occupancy of over 25 years.**

### C. The New Owner of 1505 Tulane Street

The new owner of 1505 Tulane St. has **proclaimed intent to "take back" land along the shared southern boundary** and to construct a fence along the entire northern border of 1501 Tulane St., which would block significant natural light from the home. The proposed development at 1505 Tulane St. is the subject of the COA applications at issue.
