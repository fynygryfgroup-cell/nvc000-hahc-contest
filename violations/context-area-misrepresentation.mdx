The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require new construction to be compatible in height, scale, and massing with the typical existing contributing structures in the context area. To satisfy this requirement, an applicant must accurately characterize the context area. The COA submittals for 1505 Tulane Street do not. The context area pages label neighboring properties as "2 STY" — but three of the four listed properties are not full two-story buildings. Two are camelbacks, one is a genuine one-story with no camelback element at all, and the one genuine full two-story is on a different block and predates the historic board's existence. At the same time, the applicant omitted a full two-story contributing structure that actually shares a plat line with the subject property. This selective framing creates a false picture of the neighborhood and makes the proposed full two-story massing appear compatible when the Tulane block is, in reality, composed of one-story homes and camelbacks.

<Warning>
**MAJOR FINDING — The COA submittals misrepresent the context area to justify the proposed full 2-story massing.** The context area pages of both submittals (A01, Sheet 11; A02, Sheet 9) list four neighboring properties, all designated as "Contributing – 2 STY" [28][29][30][31]. The "2 STY" label obscures a critical architectural distinction: three of the four listed properties are not full 2-story buildings. The applicant's proposed design is a full 2-story — fundamentally different in scale, massing, and character from the camelbacks that actually define this block.
</Warning>

## Section V.J — Context Area Misrepresentation: The Camelback Distinction

### The Context Area as Submitted

The context area pages of both COA submittals list the following properties, all labeled "Contributing – 2 STY":

| Address | Submitted Label | Actual Building Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1501 Tulane | Contributing – 2 STY | **Camelback** (1-story bungalow with subordinate 2-story rear portion) |
| 1511 Tulane | Contributing – 2 STY | **True 1-story** — no camelback at all |
| 1515 Tulane | Contributing – 2 STY | **Camelback** (1-story bungalow with subordinate 2-story rear portion) |
| 1530 Ashland | Contributing – 2 STY | Full 2-story — but on a **different block**, built **before the historic board existed** |

### Understanding the Camelback Distinction

The "2 STY" label obscures a critical architectural distinction. **1501 and 1515 Tulane are camelbacks** — a specific, historically appropriate Houston Heights form consisting of a **1-story bungalow with a smaller, subordinate 2-story rear portion**. The 2-story portion is set back and reduced in scale relative to the primary 1-story mass. A camelback is predominantly a 1-story building.

**1511 Tulane is a true 1-story with no camelback at all** — completely mislabeled as "2 STY."

**1530 Ashland is a full 2-story** but is on a different block and was built before the historic board even existed, making it an improper comparable for a historic district context analysis.

### Why This Matters — The Applicant's Proposed Massing Is NOT a Camelback

The applicant's proposed design is a **full 2-story rear addition and a full 2-story garage apartment** — not a camelback. A camelback is a subordinate, smaller 2-story element behind a dominant 1-story primary form. The applicant's proposed 2-story mass is full-scale, with a **21′-6″ eave height** and a **27′-7″ ridge height**, extending the full width and depth of the addition. This is fundamentally different in scale, massing, and character from a camelback.

By labeling camelbacks as simply "2 STY," the applicant creates a false equivalence between:

1. **Historic camelbacks** (predominantly 1-story with a subordinate 2-story rear bump), and
2. **The proposed full 2-story massing** (a full-scale 2-story structure extending the full width and depth)

This false equivalence makes the applicant's full 2-story design appear compatible with the context area, when in fact the context area consists of **1-story homes and camelbacks** — not full 2-story buildings.

## The Proper Context Area Analysis Under the Heights Design Guidelines

Under the Houston Heights Historic District Design Guidelines:

> *"The height of new construction must not be taller than the typical height of existing contributing structures in the context area."* [32]

> *"The scale and proportions of the new construction… must be compatible with the typical scale and proportions of existing contributing structures in the context area."* [33]

> *"A new construction shall not be constructed with more than one story in a historic district that is comprised entirely of one-story contributing structures."* [34]

When the context area is accurately described:

- The Tulane block consists of **1-story homes and camelbacks** — not full 2-story buildings.
- The "typical height" is **1-story** (camelbacks are predominantly 1-story with a subordinate rear element).
- The only full 2-story (1530 Ashland) is on a different block and predates the historic board.
- The applicant's full 2-story massing is **not compatible** with a context area of 1-story homes and camelbacks.

### Key Findings — Exhibit F

**The "2 STY" label obscures the camelback distinction.** 1501 and 1515 Tulane are camelbacks — a specific, historically appropriate Houston Heights form consisting of a 1-story bungalow with a smaller, subordinate 2-story rear portion. A camelback is predominantly a 1-story building. 1511 Tulane is a true 1-story with no camelback at all. Only 1530 Ashland is a full 2-story.

**The applicant's proposed massing is NOT a camelback.** The applicant's design is a full 2-story rear addition and a full 2-story garage apartment — with a 21′-6″ eave height and 27′-7″ ridge height extending the full width and depth. A camelback is a subordinate, smaller 2-story element behind a dominant 1-story primary form. By labeling camelbacks as simply "2 STY," the applicant creates a false equivalence between historic camelbacks and the proposed full 2-story massing.

**1530 Ashland was cherry-picked from a different block.** The only genuine full 2-story in the context area is on Ashland Street — a different block from the Tulane properties. It was built before the historic board even existed, making it an improper comparable for a historic district context analysis.

**The Tulane block consists of 1-story homes and camelbacks.** With 1530 Ashland properly excluded as a non-comparable from a different block, the actual context area on the Tulane block is 1-story homes and camelbacks — not full 2-story buildings. The proposed full 2-story massing is not compatible with this context.

## Omission of a Full 2-Story Contributing Structure Behind the Property

Additionally, a **full 2-story contributing structure** located behind 1501 Tulane St. — sharing a northern plat line with 1505 Tulane St. — has been omitted entirely from the context area documentation. The context map shows the lots directly behind 1505 Tulane (along Ashland Street, including 1504 and 1506 Ashland), but no photo, text description, or contributing/non-contributing status is provided for these properties.

This omission is significant because:

1. The HAHC requires a **complete and accurate context area analysis**. Omitting an adjacent contributing structure deprives the Commission of information needed to evaluate compatibility.
2. The selective inclusion of only 1530 Ashland (a full 2-story from a different block) while omitting a full 2-story structure that **actually shares a plat line with the subject property** further suggests the context area was curated to support the applicant's massing claims rather than to accurately represent the neighborhood.
3. The applicant's own photos contradict their labels. The HAHC can verify this by looking at the context area photographs — the photos of 1501, 1511, and 1515 Tulane clearly show **1-story primary forms** (with camelback rear portions visible on 1501 and 1515), not full 2-story buildings.

---

## Conclusion

<Warning>
When the context area is accurately described — with camelbacks labeled as camelbacks, the genuine 1-story labeled as 1-story, and the improper non-comparable from a different block excluded — the Tulane block consists entirely of **1-story homes and camelbacks**. The applicant's proposed full 2-story massing is fundamentally incompatible with this context under the Houston Heights Design Guidelines. The misrepresentation of the context area is not a labeling technicality; it is the foundation of the applicant's massing justification, and that foundation does not hold.
</Warning>
