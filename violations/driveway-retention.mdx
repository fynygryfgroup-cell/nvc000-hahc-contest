Historic driveways are a contributing element of the Houston Heights streetscape. The Houston Heights Design Guidelines recognize this by explicitly requiring that existing concrete or asphalt driveways on historic properties be retained during renovation. At 1505 Tulane Street, the property has an existing concrete or asphalt driveway — a characteristic feature of historic Houston Heights properties. The proposed plans do not retain this driveway. Instead, the new plan reconfigures site circulation, eliminates the historic driveway pattern, and replaces it with a new layout that does not preserve the existing paved surface. This is a direct violation of the Design Guidelines and one of several independent grounds for the Commission to deny or defer these applications.

## Section V.J-1 — Driveway Retention Violation: Existing Driveway Not Retained

**NEW FINDING —** The Houston Heights Design Guidelines require historic homes with existing concrete or asphalt driveways to retain those driveways during renovation. The Design Guidelines specify that pavement and driveways are included in lot coverage calculations and that existing driveways on historic properties should be retained as part of the historic character of the district [35].

The subject property at 1505 Tulane St. has an existing concrete/asphalt driveway — a characteristic feature of historic Houston Heights properties. The proposed plans do not retain this existing driveway. Instead, the new plan reconfigures the site circulation, eliminating the historic driveway pattern and replacing it with a new layout that does not preserve the existing paved surface.

This is a direct violation of the Design Guidelines' directive to retain existing driveways on historic properties during renovation. The HAHC should require the applicant to either retain the existing driveway or demonstrate why retention is infeasible — and if infeasible, to design the replacement in a manner consistent with the historic character of the district.

## Section V.J-2 — Front Parking Pad and Rear Access Rules

The Houston Heights Design Guidelines also contain specific rules regarding parking configuration in the historic district:

**Front parking pads are discouraged.** Front parking pads are discouraged in the Houston Heights Historic District, as they disrupt the historic streetscape pattern and front yard character.

**Rear access is the preferred access pattern.** Rear access is the preferred access pattern for garages in the historic district.

**Alley-loading garage clearance requirements.** The Design Guidelines specify that an alley-loading garage generally must establish a minimum of 20 feet of clearance from an opposing alley-loading garage door, the rear wall of a front-facing garage, or a fence; a **24-foot clearance is preferred** [36].

**Front-facing garage rear setback.** A front-facing garage located with its rear wall at the alley may have a **zero-foot rear setback** [37].

The HAHC should verify whether the proposed garage and parking configuration complies with these rear access and parking pad requirements, particularly given that the project spans two lots and the garage's relationship to the alley and opposing structures is affected by the lot configuration dispute.

<Info>
The driveway retention violation is one of several independent grounds for denial or deferral of the COA applications. It does not depend on the boundary dispute, the lot combination question, or the setback calculations. It is a straightforward violation of a Design Guidelines directive that applies to any historic property with an existing driveway.
</Info>

## HAHC Action Requested

The HAHC should:

1. **Require the applicant to retain the existing driveway** as required by the Houston Heights Design Guidelines.
2. **If retention is claimed to be infeasible**, require the applicant to demonstrate why — and require any replacement design to be consistent with the historic character of the district.
3. **Verify compliance with parking configuration rules**, including front parking pad discouragements and rear access requirements, given the reconfigured site layout.
4. **Confirm alley-loading garage clearance compliance**, which is affected by the unresolved lot configuration dispute.
